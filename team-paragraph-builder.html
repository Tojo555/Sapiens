The finished essay

Out of all the animals that have ever lived on Earth, Homo sapiens is the only one that went on to build cities, write books, and send rockets into space. Early humans were not the strongest or the fastest creatures in their environment, so how did they end up taking over the planet? Harari argues that three changes — mastering fire, inventing shared stories, and developing flexible skills for daily survival — gave Sapiens an edge no other species had.

Taken together, these three changes show that Sapiens' rise to power was never really about brute strength. It was about adaptability — the ability to harness new tools, invent powerful shared ideas, and get the most out of whatever environment they found themselves in.
