Signal Phrase Studio

A signal phrase is the short lead-in that tells your reader who the evidence comes from and why it matters. First learn what separates a poor one from a great one, then use them to build T.E.A.M. paragraphs. Drag a card (mouse or finger), or tap a card and then tap where it goes.

Part 1 — The signal phrase ladder

The same piece of evidence can be introduced in four very different ways. Each step up the ladder gives the reader more help.

Poor

No clear source, a weak verb, or no sense of why the evidence matters. The reader has to guess.

  • Here is a quote:
  • The book says that
  • They said that

Good

Names the source and uses a clear verb. Solid, but it doesn't say how the evidence helps your argument.

  • According to Harari,
  • Harari explains that
  • In Sapiens, Harari writes that

Great

Names the source, uses a precise verb, and shows what the evidence is doing for your claim.

  • Harari backs up this claim with a striking example:
  • To show how powerful fire was, Harari points out that

Great + transitional

Everything a great one does, plus a link back to the sentence before it, so the paragraph glides.

  • Even more surprising, Harari reveals that
  • Because of this, Harari explains that
The recipe: who is speaking + a precise verb + why the evidence is here. For a transitional signal phrase, add one more ingredient: a link back to the idea you just finished.

Pick the verb that matches the job

argues for a claim · illustrates for an example · reveals for a surprising fact · warns for a danger · challenges for pushing back · points out for a detail the reader might miss

Adds on

Building on this, · Just as importantly, · On top of that, · Beyond that,

Contrasts

Yet · Even so, · In contrast, · However,

Shows a result

Because of this, · As a result, · This led to…

Gives an example

To illustrate this idea, · For instance, · To see this in action,

Part 3 — Make it transitional

A transitional signal phrase has to do two jobs at once: introduce the new evidence and connect it to the sentence before it. Read the paragraph so far, figure out how the new evidence relates to it (adds on, contrasts, shows a result, or gives an example), and choose the phrase that fits.
